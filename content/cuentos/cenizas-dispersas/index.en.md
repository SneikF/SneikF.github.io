She watches him on the hospital bed from the doorway, three meters away, and once again the urge to come closer throbs in her.

"You won't believe me, but when I take off the last bandage there will be nothing left of me... maybe just ashes," she tells him as she approaches.

She has two meters of bandage left.

One meter.

He answers only with his eyes, and he isn't sure whether she understands them, watery and aching.

"I think it impresses you that I've come this far. I know this is my last wrapping, but you are well worth the risk." She comes closer, feeling a sharp longing to caress his chest, his shoulders. An electric feeling runs through him, perhaps fear. For her, it seems only a minute goes by; for him, it may have been hours.

Back in her sarcophagus, under bandages dusty with centuries, when she made up her mind to risk everything to feel him on her skin, she knew that touching him would be a harmful remedy. But she could not tell whether what drove her was the intense effect of an unbearable loneliness or the irresistible pull he had on her. She did not stop to think about contradictions.

So, to make him fall in love or to calm him, she now tells him that she has watched him walk past the antique shop for eleven or fourteen years. And she recites the reasons she muttered over and over in her head before coming: <<That after hearing the news that you were in the hospital, all wrapped up and in a vegetative state, I didn't think twice, and only when I walked into the room did I realize I had just one bandage left on, but that no longer mattered. That you must love me, because we already loved each other in other times. That I have a theory that says all people were, are and will be the same, that there is no point in looking for other loves, that you already loved me madly and without limits. That those memories are mine alone, mine and history's. That how can it be that luck has repeated itself and the last thing I do as a sign of affection is to lose you. That no, and no, I won't let you look at me like that again, that I already lost you once when I stayed wrapped in these rags, that now it will be you who must sacrifice yourself for me.>>

"Let me kiss you one last time. Let the little that is left of me feel how much you are."

And then it happened: she came closer slowly as the last bandage came loose; the centimeters became dangerous millimeters, then nanometers, and the distance was denied by a tangential kiss. He felt nothing more than a brush of lips, for in his lap there was nothing left but ashes, scattered ashes.

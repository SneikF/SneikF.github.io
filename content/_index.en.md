It has been a couple of decades since the one writing this was born, apparently at night. He is a self-absorbed young man, and so he is seldom heard speaking; he says what he must say and no more than that. He is often seen walking hunched over, and the same happens when he is seen sitting; he says it is a trace left by his wish to get inside whatever he is reading or writing. He also adds how rewarding he finds losing himself in solving mathematical and computational problems; he is not discouraged by the fact that today's machines do it in a fraction of a second, since he simply likes solving problems. His aspirations in the world are aimed at understanding, and at making something practical and real out of it.

At first he seems to carry an apathetic character; it is only a sign of his preference for authentic and honest relationships. He has ventured into the world of abstraction and has learned to deal with rigor and frustration; he has grown used to the rigid, detached manner of his teachers, as well as to the warm, attentive care of his friends and family. The writer mentions how stimulating it would be for him to apply his creativity and his way of thinking; part of his happiness would consist in being useful to the world through what he thinks and does, and his misfortune would lie in achieving the opposite. He cannot stay any longer in that limbo that is inaction; he wants to keep walking with determination.

He loves to read.
